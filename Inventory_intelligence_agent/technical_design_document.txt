# IntelliSupply Planning Agent – Technical Design Document

## Overview

The IntelliSupply Planning Agent is an AI-assisted inventory planning and scenario simulation platform. It combines historical demand analysis, inventory health monitoring, demand forecasting, replenishment planning, risk scoring, Monte Carlo simulation, and discrete event simulation (DES) to help supply chain teams evaluate inventory decisions before execution.

The system uses precomputed analytics generated in an offline data pipeline and runtime simulations to answer "what-if" planning questions such as:

* What happens if demand increases by 25% next week?
* What if replenishment is delayed by 5 days?
* Should inventory be transferred from another hub?
* Will an emergency reorder prevent a stockout?

---

# Architecture Overview

The solution is divided into two major layers:

## Offline Analytics Pipeline

The offline pipeline generates all foundational intelligence required by the planning engine.

### Stage 0 – Dataset Preparation

Produces:

* planning_dataset.csv

### Stage 1 – Demand Analysis

Produces:

* demand_daily.csv
* demand_analysis_daily.csv

Calculates:

* Rolling demand averages
* Demand growth trends
* Demand volatility
* Year-over-year comparisons

### Stage 1.5 – Forecast Model Training

Produces:

* best_demand_forecasting_model.pkl

Trains and selects the best forecasting model.

### Stage 1.6 – Future Demand Forecasting

Produces:

* demand_forecast_daily.csv

Provides:

* 30-day forecasts
* Prediction intervals
* Confidence scores

### Stage 2 – Inventory Health Analysis

Produces:

* inventory_daily_snapshot.csv
* inventory_health_daily.csv

Calculates:

* Current inventory position
* Safety stock
* Coverage days
* Velocity scores
* Inventory status

### Stage 2.5 – Replenishment Planning

Produces:

* replenishment_orders.csv

Contains:

* Open replenishment orders
* Expected arrivals
* Lead times
* Delay information

### Stage 3 – Risk Scoring

Produces:

* inventory_risk_daily.csv

Computes:

* Stock coverage risk
* Demand volatility risk
* Seasonality risk
* Replenishment delay risk
* Composite risk score

# Runtime Planning Agent

The runtime system consumes the offline outputs and performs scenario simulation.

## Stage 0 – Inventory State Construction

Purpose:
Create a unified InventoryState object by combining data from:

* inventory_health_daily
* demand_analysis_daily
* demand_forecast_daily
* replenishment_orders
* inventory_risk_daily

Outputs:

* InventoryState

Contains:

* Inventory metrics
* Demand metrics
* Forecast metrics
* Replenishment metrics
* Risk metrics

---

## Stage 1 – Scenario Understanding

Purpose:
Convert natural language user requests into structured parameters.

Example:

Input:
"What if demand increases by 25% next week?"

Output:
{
"demand_multiplier": 1.25,
"planning_window_days": 7
}

Extracted Parameters:

* demand_multiplier
* lead_time_delta_days
* transfer_quantity
* reorder_quantity
* promotion_active
* planning_window_days

---

## Stage 2 – Scenario State Construction

Purpose:
Apply user-defined scenario changes to the InventoryState.

Examples:

* Increase demand forecasts
* Delay replenishment arrivals
* Add transfer inventory
* Create emergency orders

Outputs:

* ScenarioState

---

## Stage 3 – Monte Carlo World Generation

Purpose:
Generate thousands of realistic future worlds.

Variables sampled:

* Demand uncertainty
* Forecast error
* Replenishment delay variance

Variables held fixed:

* User scenario assumptions

Outputs:

* Simulated future worlds

---

## Stage 4 – Discrete Event Simulation (DES)

Purpose:
Simulate inventory movement day by day.

Simulation events:

* Inventory consumption
* Order arrivals
* Stockouts
* Safety stock breaches

Outputs:

* Final inventory
* Shortages
* Stockout timing
* Service performance

---

## Stage 5 – Outcome Discovery

Purpose:
Aggregate simulation results into understandable outcomes.

Generated outcomes:

* Most Likely Outcome
* Best Case Outcome
* Worst Case Outcome

Metrics:

* Stockout probability
* Shortage volume
* Remaining inventory

---

## Stage 6 – Decision Generation

Purpose:
Generate actionable interventions.

Examples:

* Transfer inventory
* Emergency reorder
* Increase safety stock
* Do nothing

Generated from:

* Current inventory state
* Hub availability
* Risk conditions

---

## Stage 7 – Decision Simulation

Purpose:
Evaluate the effect of a selected intervention.

Process:

1. Apply decision
2. Generate new worlds
3. Run simulations again
4. Compare before vs after

Outputs:

* Updated outcome probabilities
* Inventory improvements
* Risk reduction

---

## Stage 8 – Utility Scoring

Purpose:
Assign a single effectiveness score to a decision.

Factors:

* Stockout reduction
* Shortage reduction
* Coverage improvement
* Cost penalty

Output:

* Utility Score (0–100)

---

## Stage 9 – LLM Explanation Layer

Purpose:
Translate simulation outputs into business language.

Responsibilities:

* Explain risks
* Summarize outcomes
* Describe intervention effects
* Provide recommendations

Constraints:

* Must not invent numbers
* Must use simulation outputs only

---

# Core Runtime Data Sources

The planning agent relies on five operational tables:

1. inventory_health_daily
2. demand_analysis_daily
3. demand_forecast_daily
4. replenishment_orders
5. inventory_risk_daily

These tables provide all inventory, demand, forecast, replenishment, and risk information required to build runtime states.

---

# Key Design Principles

1. Analytics are precomputed offline.
2. Runtime operations are lightweight and fast.
3. User scenarios remain deterministic.
4. Uncertainty is modeled through Monte Carlo simulation.
5. Business decisions are evaluated through DES.
6. LLMs explain results rather than generate them.
7. All recommendations are simulation-backed and auditable.

---

# Expected User Workflow

1. User asks a planning question.
2. Inventory state is assembled.
3. Scenario parameters are extracted.
4. Scenario state is created.
5. Monte Carlo worlds are generated.
6. DES simulations are executed.
7. Outcomes are discovered.
8. Decisions are generated.
9. User selects a decision.
10. Decision is re-simulated.
11. Utility is calculated.
12. LLM explains results.

Result:
A defensible, simulation-driven inventory planning recommendation.
